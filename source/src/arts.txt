ts: Reaching Higher (and Lower) || Rehearsal || How do I extend my range safely?
tx: Principles: ¶ • High notes need faster air and a firm, not pinched, embouchure. ¶ • Low notes need relaxed, open, warm air. ¶ • Never force high notes by pressing the mouthpiece harder into the lips (brass) or biting (woodwinds). ¶ • Rest as much as you play. ¶ Part 1, Warm-up (5 minutes): Long tones in the middle register. ¶ Part 2, Flexibility (15 minutes): ¶ • Brass: lip slurs on one valve combination, such as concert B♭ to F and back, then up to the next partial. Keep the air steady and let the lips do small adjustments. ¶ • Woodwinds: register slurs (low to high with the register or octave key), and long tones at the top and bottom of the comfortable range. ¶ • Flute: harmonic exercises (overblowing a low fingering to sound higher partials). ¶ • Percussion: expand dynamic "range" with very soft to very loud strokes, and four-mallet or timpani tuning if available. ¶ Part 3, Extend by one note (10 minutes): Play a scale up to your highest comfortable note, rest, then try one note higher with a big, fast breath. hook: Professional musicians often practise a hard passage at half speed, perfectly, many times, before playing it at tempo. It seems slow, but it's the fastest way to get it right. In Instrumental Music 30, you'll learn to practise like a professional.

v: chunking :: Breaking a passage into small sections.
v: rhythm variation :: Practising a passage in altered rhythms (such as long-short) to build control.

ts: Practise Like a Pro || Rehearsal || How do professionals master difficult passages?
tx: Step 1, Find the problem (5 minutes): Play through your concert piece and mark the two hardest passages with brackets. ¶ Step 2, Diagnose (5 minutes): Is the problem notes, rhythm, fingering, tonguing, breathing or endurance? Write it down. ¶ Step 3, Chunk (10 minutes): Break the passage into chunks of one or two beats. Practise each chunk, then link chunk 1 to the first note of chunk 2 ("chaining"). ¶ Step 4, Slow practice (10 minutes): Set the metronome at half the target tempo. Play the passage 3 times in a row perfectly before moving the metronome up by 4–8 beats per minute. A mistake means going back down. ¶ Step 5, Rhythm variations (5 minutes): Play the passage long-short, then short-long, then in triplets. This strengthens weak finger or stick combinations. ¶ Step 6, Context (5 minutes): Play from four bars before the passage to four bars after, so it connects musically. ¶ Practice log: date, passage, starting tempo, ending tempo, what improved. ¶ Professional habit: Stop practising a passage while it's still going well, rather than when you're tired and making mistakes.
