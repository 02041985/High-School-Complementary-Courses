hook: Before the printing press, every image had to be made by hand, one at a time. Printmaking changed that: carve a block once and you can pull dozens of prints. Artists from Japanese ukiyo-e masters to Inuit printmakers in Kinngait (Cape Dorset) have used it to share images with the world.

v: relief print :: A print made by carving away areas so the raised surface takes the ink.
v: monoprint :: A one-of-a-kind print made from a painted or inked smooth plate.
v: brayer :: A small roller used to spread ink evenly.
v: edition :: A set of identical prints from the same plate.
v: registration :: Lining up the paper and plate so each print is in the same place.
v: positive and negative space :: The printed (raised) areas and the carved-away areas.
ts: Carve, Ink, Print || Studio task || How does printmaking change the way an artist designs an image?
tx: Materials: soft-cut lino or foam blocks, lino cutters with guards, bench hooks, water-based block ink, brayers, inking plates, wooden spoons or a press, paper. ¶ Safety: Always carve away from your body with the block held in a bench hook. Keep your free hand behind the blade. ¶ Part 1, Design (15 minutes): Draw a design the size of the block. Use strong contrast: large black and white shapes. Remember that the print will be reversed, so text must be written backward. Shade areas you will NOT carve. ¶ Part 2, Transfer and carve (30 minutes): Transfer the design by rubbing a pencil drawing face-down onto the block. Carve the white areas away using a V-gouge for lines and a U-gouge for clearing. ¶ Part 3, Ink and print (20 minutes): Roll a thin, even layer of ink on the plate until it sounds like velvet. Ink the block, place the paper using a registration jig and burnish with a spoon. Pull a proof. ¶ Part 4, Edition (15 minutes): Make adjustments, then pull an edition of three to five. Sign in pencil below the image: edition number (for example 2/5), title, signature, year. ¶ Monoprint option: Paint directly onto a smooth plexiglass plate and print once.
