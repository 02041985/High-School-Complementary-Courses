ts: Art That Asks Questions || Case study || How do contemporary Canadian and Indigenous artists use art to explore issues?
tx: Contemporary art strategies: appropriation (reusing familiar images), humour and irony, performance, installation, new media and collaboration with communities. ¶ Artist 1, Kent Monkman (Cree): Paints in the grand style of 19th-century history painting and inserts his alter ego, Miss Chief, to challenge colonial stories about Indigenous peoples. Example: "The Scream" (2017) depicts children being taken to residential schools. ¶ Artist 2, Christi Belcourt (Métis): Large paintings that look like traditional Métis beadwork, showing plants and their relationships; she also led "Walking With Our Sisters," a memorial to missing and murdered Indigenous women and girls. ¶ Artist 3, Annie Pootoogook (Inuit, Kinngait): Coloured-pencil drawings of everyday modern Inuit life (television, kitchens, shopping), changing outsiders' ideas of "Inuit art." ¶ Artist 4, Edward Burtynsky (Toronto): Large photographs of industrial landscapes (mines, oil sands, quarries) that are beautiful and disturbing at the same time. ¶ Task: In groups, analyze one artist: strategy, issue, how form supports the message, and audience response. hook: A university portfolio reviewer may spend only a few minutes on your work. In that time they need to see who you are as an artist. Curating means choosing what to leave out as much as what to put in.
sc: I can select works that show range and a strong personal voice | I can sequence and present a portfolio | I can document work with clear photos and labels
v: portfolio :: A curated collection of an artist's best work.
v: curate :: To select and arrange works for a purpose and audience.
v: sequence :: The order in which works are presented.
v: documentation :: Photos and information that record an artwork.
v: process work :: Sketches and studies showing how an artwork developed.
v: exhibition :: A public display of artworks.
ts: Curate Your Best || Studio task || How do artists choose and present their strongest work?
tx: Part 1, Inventory (15 minutes): Lay out or list every artwork from Grades 10 to 12. Rate each from 1 to 4 on skill, idea and personal voice. ¶ Part 2, Know the audience (5 minutes): Portfolios for art schools often ask for 10 to 20 works, mostly from observation plus some personal projects and sketchbook pages. Exhibitions need a theme and space plan. ¶ Part 3, Select (15 minutes): Choose 12 works that show: drawing from observation, range of media, a series or theme, and at least two process pages. Remove weaker pieces even if they took a long time. ¶ Part 4, Sequence (10 minutes): Start and end with your strongest works. Group related pieces. ¶ Part 5, Document (15 minutes): Photograph each work square-on in even, natural light with no glare, cropped to the edges. Label: title, medium, size, year. Write one or two sentences per work.
