v: simple metre :: A metre where each beat divides into two (2/4, 3/4, 4/4).
v: compound metre :: A metre where each beat divides into three (6/8, 9/8, 12/8).
v: dotted quarter beat :: In compound metre, the beat is a dotted quarter note (three eighths).
v: mixed metre :: Music where the time signature changes during the piece.
v: asymmetric metre :: A metre with uneven beat groupings, such as 5/4 or 7/8.
v: conducting pattern :: The shape a conductor draws to show the beats in a bar.
ts: Feeling in Threes || Rhythm workshop || How do musicians count and feel compound and mixed metres?
tx: Part 1, Simple vs compound (10 minutes): 3/4 = three quarter-note beats, each dividing in two ("1 & 2 & 3 &"). 6/8 = six eighth notes grouped 3 + 3, felt as two dotted-quarter beats ("1-la-li 2-la-li"). Count both aloud while clapping. They have the same number of eighths but feel totally different. ¶ Part 2, 9/8 and 12/8 (10 minutes): 9/8 = three dotted-quarter beats (3 + 3 + 3); 12/8 = four (3 + 3 + 3 + 3), the shuffle feel in blues. ¶ Part 3, Asymmetric (10 minutes): 5/4 grouped 3 + 2 ("1 2 3 1 2"); 7/8 grouped 2 + 2 + 3 or 3 + 2 + 2. Clap the accents. ¶ Part 4, Conducting (5 minutes): Conduct 6/8 in two, 9/8 in three. ¶ Part 5, Mixed metre etude (15 minutes): Play a short exercise that changes 4/4, 6/8, 5/4, 4/4. Rule: in many band pieces the eighth note stays the same speed across changes; check the marking (for example eighth = eighth).

hook: In a professional orchestra, the principal of each section leads without saying much: a nod, a breath, a lifted bell. In a high school band, section leaders run sectionals, fix problems and help the whole group get better faster.
sc: I can plan and lead a short sectional rehearsal | I can identify and fix problems with clear, kind feedback | I can play independently in a small ensemble without a conductor
v: section leader :: The player who leads and guides their section.
v: sectional :: A rehearsal of one section without the full band.
v: rehearsal plan :: A list of goals and steps for a rehearsal.
v: chamber ensemble :: A small group with one player per part, often without a conductor.
v: cue :: A visual signal (breath, nod, instrument movement) to start or change together.
v: independence :: Playing your part confidently without relying on others.
ts: Leading from the Chair || Leadership workshop || How do section leaders and chamber players rehearse without a conductor?
tx: Part 1, Plan (10 minutes): Section leaders choose 16 bars from a band piece. Plan a 20-minute sectional: tune (2 minutes), identify the two hardest spots, fix them (slow, chunk, then in context), run the 16 bars, set one goal for home practice. ¶ Part 2, Lead (20 minutes): Leaders run the sectional. Feedback rules: name the bar, name the problem, give a fix ("Bar 23, the eighths are rushing; let's play it at 60 with the metronome"). Praise something specific. ¶ Part 3, Chamber skills (15 minutes): Form trios or quartets (one per part). Choose a leader who gives the cue: a big breath and a nod on the upbeat before the first note. Practise starting together, ending together (watch the leader's instrument lift and drop), and changing tempo. ¶ Part 4, Reflect (5 minutes): What did the leader do that helped? What will you do differently next time?
