hook: A Bach chorale, a Sousa march and a Duke Ellington chart can all be played by the same band, but if you play them all the same way, you'll get all three wrong. Stylistic accuracy starts with knowing where a piece comes from.
sc: I can research a work's composer, period and style | I can apply correct articulation, tone and feel for the style | I can write a programme note that informs an audience
v: style :: The characteristic way music of a period, place or genre is played.
v: articulation :: How notes are started and connected (legato, staccato, accent, marcato).
v: programme note :: A short text in a concert program telling the audience about a piece.
v: period :: A time in music history, such as Baroque or Romantic.
v: march style :: Crisp, separated notes with steady tempo and strong accents.
v: big band style :: Jazz style with swing feel, punchy accents and section unity.
ts: Know the Piece || Research and performance task || How does research help an ensemble play in the right style?
tx: Part 1, Research (20 minutes): For one piece in your concert folder, find: composer and dates; when and why it was written; the period or genre; key features of that style; one recording by a respected ensemble. ¶ Part 2, Style sheet (15 minutes): Make a style sheet. Examples: ¶ • March (Sousa, early 1900s): notes slightly separated, crisp accents, steady tempo, dynamics change at the trio (often softer first time), bold low brass. ¶ • Chorale (Bach or band chorale): legato, connected air, balance toward the bass, breathe together at phrase ends. ¶ • Big band swing (Ellington, Basie): swing eighths, short "dot" quarters, accents on off-beats, falls and doits, sections play as one. ¶ • Contemporary concert band (for example Eric Whitacre or Kelly-Marie Murphy): extended techniques, clusters, attention to colour and space. ¶ Part 3, Apply (15 minutes): Rehearse 16 bars using the style sheet. Record before and after. ¶ Part 4, Programme note (10 minutes): Write 100 to 150 words for the concert program: who, when, why, what to listen for. No spoilers that would bore the audience; give them something to notice.

hook: Two professional players can play the same solo with the same notes, and one makes the audience lean forward while the other doesn't. The difference is interpretation: the choices you make about what the music means and how to show it.
sc: I can make and justify interpretive choices in a solo | I can prepare a solo and excerpts for an audition | I can use strategies to manage performance anxiety
v: interpretation :: The performer's choices about how to express a piece.
v: rubato :: Flexible tempo, stretching and pushing time for expression.
v: excerpt :: A short passage from a larger work, often used in auditions.
v: audition :: A performance to be selected for an ensemble, program or role.
v: performance anxiety :: Nervousness before or during a performance.
v: mock audition :: A practice audition that simulates the real experience.
ts: Make It Yours || Performance preparation || How do musicians interpret a solo and prepare for an audition?
tx: Part 1, Interpretive map (15 minutes): For your solo, mark: the overall character (for example "nostalgic," "playful"), each phrase's high point, places for rubato, colour changes (bright or dark tone), and the climax of the whole piece. Justify each choice with evidence from the score (harmony, range, markings) or the composer's style. ¶ Part 2, Compare (10 minutes): Listen to two professional recordings of the same piece. Note two differences in interpretation. Which choices do you agree with? ¶ Part 3, Audition prep (10 minutes): Typical audition: a prepared solo, scales, orchestral or band excerpts, and sight-reading. Make a two-week plan: daily scales, excerpt work slow to fast, solo run-throughs, weekly recording. ¶ Part 4, Managing anxiety (10 minutes): Strategies: slow breathing (in 4, hold 4, out 6), a pre-performance routine, focusing on musical intentions rather than mistakes, positive self-talk, practising under pressure. Remember: some nerves give energy. ¶ Part 5, Mock audition (15 minutes): Perform for a small panel of classmates who give one strength and one suggestion.
