hook: In China, a common greeting isn't "How are you?" but 你吃了吗？Nǐ chī le ma? "Have you eaten?" Food is at the heart of Chinese culture, and the particle 了 le helps you talk about what you've done.

ts: 吃了吗？Chī le ma? || Grammar and culture || How do I talk about health, food and completed actions in Mandarin?
tx: Healthy habits: 多吃蔬菜 duō chī shūcài (eat more vegetables), 多喝水 duō hē shuǐ (drink more water), 每天运动 měitiān yùndòng (exercise every day), 早睡早起 zǎo shuì zǎo qǐ (early to bed, early to rise), 少吃糖 shǎo chī táng (eat less sugar). ¶ 了 le for completed actions: verb + 了: 我吃了早饭。Wǒ chī le zǎofàn. = I ate breakfast. Negative uses 没 méi without 了: 我没吃早饭。Wǒ méi chī zǎofàn. = I didn't eat breakfast. Question: 你吃了吗？/ 你吃了没有？ ¶ Food culture: Dishes are shared from the centre of the table; each person has a bowl of rice. Use 筷子 kuàizi; never stick chopsticks upright in rice (it resembles incense at funerals). Hot tea is common with meals. Regional cuisines: 四川 Sìchuān (spicy), 广东 Guǎngdōng (dim sum), 北京 Běijīng (roast duck). ¶ 对话: ¶ 小明 Xiǎomíng：你吃了吗？Nǐ chī le ma? ¶ 莉莉 Lìlì：我吃了。我吃了米饭和蔬菜，还喝了茶。Wǒ chī le. Wǒ chī le mǐfàn hé shūcài, hái hē le chá. ¶ 小明：你很健康！我今天没吃早饭。Nǐ hěn jiànkāng! Wǒ jīntiān méi chī zǎofàn. ¶ 莉莉：不好！你应该每天吃早饭。Bù hǎo!
