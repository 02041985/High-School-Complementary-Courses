hook: A carpenter's saying goes: "Measure twice, cut once." In every CTS area, from foods to construction to fashion, the plan is what turns materials into a finished product without wasting time or money.
sc: I can read a project plan and materials list | I can measure accurately and follow steps in order | I can track my progress and solve problems as I work
v: project plan :: A document listing the steps, materials and tools needed to complete a project.
v: materials list :: A list of every material and quantity needed.
v: procedure :: Step-by-step instructions in order.
v: measurement :: Finding size, amount or quantity using tools such as rulers or measuring cups.
v: tolerance :: The allowable difference from an exact measurement.
v: finish :: The final surface treatment or presentation of a product.
ts: Follow the Plan || Lab or shop task || How do I follow a plan to complete a quality project?
tx: Choose a starter project for your CTS area (teacher provides the plan): ¶ • Foods: a batch of muffins (recipe = plan) ¶ • Construction: a small wooden box or bookend ¶ • Fashion: a drawstring bag ¶ • Design or communications: a printed event poster from a template ¶ • Computing: a simple webpage from a wireframe ¶ Part 1, Read the whole plan (10 minutes): Before touching anything, read every step. Highlight safety notes, circle unfamiliar words and look them up. ¶ Part 2, Gather (10 minutes): Check off each item on the materials and tools list. Mise en place (everything in place) is used by chefs and works in every shop. ¶ Part 3, Measure (10 minutes): Practise with the right tools: dry vs liquid measuring cups, tape measure to 1 mm, seam allowance gauge. Know the tolerance: a box joint may allow ±1 mm, a muffin recipe ±5 g of flour. ¶ Part 4, Build (40 minutes or across classes): Follow steps in order. Tick each step on a progress log with the time and any problems. ¶ Part 5, Finish and clean (10 minutes): Apply the finish, clean the work area and return tools.

hook: Before a product reaches a store shelf, someone checks it against a list of requirements. In a CTS lab, that someone is you. Evaluating your own work honestly is what makes the next project better.
sc: I can use criteria and a rubric to evaluate a product | I can give and use specific feedback | I can plan realistic improvements
v: criteria :: Standards used to judge quality.
v: rubric :: A scoring guide that describes levels of quality for each criterion.
v: self-evaluation :: Judging your own work against criteria.
v: feedback :: Information about strengths and areas to improve.
v: quality :: How well a product meets its standards.
v: improvement plan :: Specific steps to make the next product better.
ts: Is It Good Enough? || Evaluation task || How do I judge my work fairly and make it better?
tx: Part 1, Criteria (10 minutes): As a class, list criteria for your project type. Example for a wooden box: accurate dimensions (±1 mm), square corners, tight joints, smooth finish with no tear-out, safe (no splinters or sharp edges). Example for muffins: even size, golden colour, domed tops, moist crumb, cooked through, clean presentation. ¶ Part 2, Rubric (10 minutes): Build a four-level rubric for each criterion: 4 Exceeds, 3 Meets, 2 Approaching, 1 Beginning. Describe what each level looks like. ¶ Part 3, Self-evaluate (10 minutes): Score your own project with evidence ("Corners measured 89° and 90°, so Meets"). ¶ Part 4, Peer feedback (15 minutes): In pairs, use "Glow and Grow": one specific strength and one specific suggestion, linked to criteria. ¶ Part 5, Improvement plan (10 minutes): Choose the two lowest criteria and write one action for each ("Use a try square before gluing to check 90°").
