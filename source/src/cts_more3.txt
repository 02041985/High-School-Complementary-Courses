hook: In the workplace, you don't build what you want; you build what the client needs, on their timeline and budget. The most important design skill at the advanced level isn't technical. It's listening.
sc: I can interview a client to find their needs | I can write clear, measurable requirements and a proposal | I can plan deliverables and deadlines
v: client :: The person or organization a project is created for.
v: requirements :: Specific, agreed needs a project must meet.
v: proposal :: A document describing the plan, cost and timeline for a client to approve.
v: deliverable :: A product or result promised to the client.
v: deadline :: The date a deliverable is due.
v: scope :: The agreed boundaries of what a project includes.
ts: Working for a Client || Client project || How do professionals turn a client's needs into a project plan?
tx: Client options: a teacher or school department (posters for an event, a catered lunch, shelving for a classroom, a website for a club), a local business or community group, or a simulated client card from the teacher. ¶ Part 1, Prepare (10 minutes): Write eight open questions. Examples: What problem are you trying to solve? Who will use it? What does success look like? What's your budget? When do you need it? What must it not be? Can you show examples you like? ¶ Part 2, Interview (15 minutes): Listen more than you talk. Take notes. Repeat back key points to confirm ("So you need 40 lunches, nut-free, by noon Friday?"). ¶ Part 3, Requirements (10 minutes): Turn notes into 5 to 8 measurable requirements and note what is out of scope. ¶ Part 4, Proposal (20 minutes): Write a one-page proposal: project summary, requirements, deliverables with deadlines, cost estimate, two design options with sketches, and a sign-off line. ¶ Scope creep: when clients add requests after agreement, record the change and its effect on cost and time before agreeing.

hook: An electrician, a Red Seal chef, a certified dental assistant and a network engineer all have something in common: a credential that tells employers they meet a recognized standard. Knowing which credential your career needs is the first step to getting it.
sc: I can distinguish certificates, diplomas, degrees and trade certification | I can research credentials and post-secondary programs for a career | I can build a career action plan
v: credential :: A qualification that shows you meet a recognized standard.
v: certification :: Official recognition, often by exam, that you meet professional standards.
v: Red Seal :: An interprovincial standard for skilled trades in Canada, letting tradespeople work across provinces.
v: diploma :: A credential usually from a college or polytechnic, typically two years.
v: degree :: A credential from a university, typically four years for a bachelor's.
v: career action plan :: A step-by-step plan to reach a career goal.
ts: Credential Quest || Career research || What credentials and programs lead to the career I want?
tx: Types of credentials: ¶ • Certificate: short program (months to one year), for example a food safety certificate or a college certificate. ¶ • Industry certification: for example Food Safe, WHMIS, CompTIA A+, Adobe Certified Professional, First Aid. ¶ • Trade certification: apprenticeship + exams leads to journeyperson certification; many trades also offer the Red Seal endorsement. ¶ • Diploma: two-year programs at polytechnics and colleges (for example NAIT and SAIT in Alberta). ¶ • Degree: university bachelor's (usually four years), then master's or professional degrees. ¶ Part 1, Choose (5 minutes): Pick one career connected to your CTS area. ¶ Part 2, Research (25 minutes): Using alis.alberta.ca and post-secondary websites, find: credential required, at least two programs that offer it (name, length, admission requirements, cost), certifications that would help, and whether Red Seal applies. ¶ Part 3, Compare (10 minutes): Compare two pathways to the same career (for example apprenticeship vs diploma). ¶ Part 4, Action plan (10 minutes): Write steps from now to employment: high school courses, certifications to get now, applications, dates.
