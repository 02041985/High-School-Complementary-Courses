ts: Choose It, Plan It, Own It || Performance preparation || How do musicians choose and prepare repertoire for performance?
tx: Part 1, Choosing (10 minutes): Use these criteria to shortlist two or three pieces: ¶ • Fit: suits my range and current technique, with one or two stretch goals ¶ • Contrast: if performing more than one piece, contrast in tempo, mood or style ¶ • Occasion: length and style suit the audience and event ¶ • Connection: something I want to say with it ¶ Part 2, Map the piece (10 minutes): Mark sections and the hardest passages (circle them). Note the form. ¶ Part 3, Practice plan (15 minutes): Plan four weeks. Each day: warm-up (5 minutes), technical work on circled passages using chunking and slow practice with a metronome, then raise the tempo by 4 bpm when clean three times in a row (15 minutes), then play through for musicality (10 minutes). Record yourself once a week. ¶ Part 4, Interpretation (10 minutes): Mark choices in the music: breaths, dynamics beyond the written ones, tempo flexibility, articulation, and the mood or story of each section. ¶ Part 5, Self-assessment (5 minutes): Rate accuracy, tone, rhythm, expression and stage presence from 1 to 4.

hook: Most hit songs are built around a hook, a short catchy idea you can't get out of your head, often in the first 30 seconds. Writing a song starts with finding that hook and building everything else to support it.
sc: I can write lyrics with a clear theme, imagery and rhyme | I can write a melody that fits the lyrics and chords | I can structure a song with verse, chorus and hook
v: hook :: A short, catchy musical or lyrical idea that grabs attention.
v: verse :: A section that tells the story; lyrics usually change each time.
v: chorus :: The repeated section that carries the main message and hook.
v: prosody :: How well the melody and rhythm fit the natural stress of the words.
v: rhyme scheme :: The pattern of rhymes, labelled with letters (AABB, ABAB).
v: imagery :: Words that create pictures and senses in the listener's mind.
ts: Hook First || Songwriting workshop || How do songwriters turn an idea into lyrics and melody?
tx: Part 1, Title and theme (10 minutes): Brainstorm 10 possible song titles from your life (a place, a phrase someone says, a feeling). Pick one that could be the hook. ¶ Part 2, Chorus lyrics (10 minutes): Write four lines that state the main idea. Put the title in the first or last line. Keep it simple and singable. Choose a rhyme scheme (AABB or ABAB). ¶ Part 3, Verse lyrics (10 minutes): Write verse 1 with concrete imagery: show, don't tell. Instead of "I was sad," try "Your jacket's still on the hook by the door." ¶ Part 4, Melody (15 minutes): Over a four-chord loop (for example C-G-Am-F), speak the chorus lyrics in rhythm, then sing them on pitches from the chords. Check prosody: stressed syllables should land on strong beats and higher or longer notes. Make the chorus melody higher or more energetic than the verse. ¶ Part 5, Structure (5 minutes): Map the song: verse 1, chorus, verse 2, chorus, bridge, chorus. Record a voice memo of the draft.
